About

The points are evidence. The arcs are inference. Every point is a real record from a published dataset; the arcs between clades are reconstructed from those records, not observed. A clade's position is a summary statistic of where its carriers were dug up, which is not the same as where it arose.

Guide

Reading the globe

Points are records. Each is a published, dated, georeferenced record; click one for its citation.

Arcs are inference. They connect a clade to its parent. A clade's date is its oldest sampled member, not when it arose, so every clade is older than the atlas can show.

Two placements disagree. Centroid and oldest-sample positions give different routes; the gap between them is how much the route depends on the statistic rather than the data.

Empty is not absent. Where the timeline's sediment thins out, nobody has dug or sequenced there yet. The Sources tab has the full coverage chart.
